Long-Term Functional Outcomes After Prehabilitation in Frail Older Adults Undergoing Colorectal Cancer Surgery: A One-Year Prospective Cohort Study.

Dobrzycka M, Wołoszyn P, Prud M, Bieniaszewski K, Spychalski P, Gierat-Haponiuk K, et al. Journal of Clinical Medicine 2026. Original study · DOI 10.3390/jcm15124731 · PubMed 42355899

Prospective observational cohort of frail patients aged over 70 years who completed structured prehabilitation before colorectal cancer surgery; 41 of 93 eligible patients completed 1-year follow-up. The 5-item Frailty Index increased slightly (mean difference 0.029, p = 0.012) with no change in the Clinical Frailty Scale, while postural function improved and functional performance remained stable (6-minute walk test 392.71 vs 384.36 m, p = 0.885; Timed Up and Go 12.36 vs 10.42 s, p = 0.051). The authors describe a dissociation between physiological vulnerability and functional status, to be interpreted descriptively given the absence of a control group.

Long-term effects of colonoscopy screening on colorectal cancer incidence and mortality: a multicountry, population-based randomised controlled trial.

Kaminski MF, Kalager M, Løberg M, Emilsson L, Macios A, Samy F, et al. (Spychalski P 18/22); NordICC Study Group. The Lancet 2026. Randomised trial · DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(26)00508-8 · PubMed 42102826

Multicountry population-based randomised trial (NordICC) of 84,583 adults aged 55 to 64 years in Norway, Poland and Sweden, allocated 1:2 to invitation to a single screening colonoscopy or no screening, reporting 13-year follow-up. Colorectal cancer incidence was reduced (intention-to-screen RR 0.81, 95% CI 0.71 to 0.90; per-protocol RR 0.55, 95% CI 0.33 to 0.81), mainly for distal cancers, whereas colorectal cancer mortality was not significantly reduced (intention-to-screen RR 0.88, 95% CI 0.68 to 1.08). Observed mortality in the control group (0.47%) was substantially lower than the 0.82% assumed at trial design.

Study of Treatment with Intensified Omeprazole to Prevent High-Output Stoma - A Protocol for a Randomized, Parallel-Group, Open-Label, Superiority Trial in Adults Undergoing Ileostomy (STOP-HOS-1).

Sylwestrzak T, Ciosek M, Połomska K, Kobiela J, Spychalski P. Journal of Clinical Medicine 2026. Trial protocol · senior (last) author · DOI 10.3390/jcm15051841 · PubMed 41827257

Protocol of STOP-HOS-1, a two-centre randomised, parallel-group, open-label superiority trial in Poland planning to enrol 100 adults undergoing loop or end ileostomy. Participants are randomised 1:1 to intensified intravenous omeprazole (80 mg loading dose, then 40 mg twice daily) or standard dosing (40 mg once daily) through postoperative day 10; the primary outcome is mean ileostomy output across postoperative days 1 to 3, with a reduction of at least 250 mL/day prespecified as clinically meaningful. Secondary outcomes include high-output stoma incidence, time to output stabilisation, dehydration-related complications, length of stay and 30-day readmission, analysed by intention to treat.

Definitive surgery for Hirschsprung Disease between 3 and 12 months achieves best outcomes: A systematic review with meta-analysis.

Sowulewski O, Leszkowicz J, Sakowska M, Spychalski P, Szlagatys-Sidorkiewicz A. Journal of Pediatric Surgery 2026. Meta-analysis · corresponding author · DOI 10.1016/j.jpedsurg.2026.163039 · PubMed 41780589

Systematic review with meta-analysis (PRISMA) of 19 studies published 1998 to 2025 including 3980 children undergoing definitive pull-through for Hirschsprung disease, stratified by age at surgery. Neonatal surgery was associated with more Hirschsprung-associated enterocolitis (22.4% vs 15.5%), anastomotic leakage (7.1% vs 1.5%), long-term faecal incontinence (30.0% vs 17.8%) and constipation (25.2% vs 12.8%); surgery before 3 months showed a similar pattern. Procedures performed during infancy after 3 months showed more balanced outcomes, informing the timing debate not settled by current guidelines.

Reliability of Transcutaneous Laryngeal Ultrasonography Performed as Point-of-Care Ultrasound (POCUS) of the Larynx: A Preliminary Prospective Cohort Study.

Patel A, Spychalski P, Kobiela J. Journal of Voice 2026. Original study · DOI 10.1016/j.jvoice.2026.04.014 · PubMed 42086448

Prospective cohort in which six ultrasound-naive medical students performed transcutaneous laryngeal ultrasonography (TLUSG) with a handheld point-of-care device (Butterfly iQ+) and a conventional system (Mindray ME8) in peri-thyroidectomy patients, using laryngoscopy as reference. Across 229 examinations per device in 96 patients, vocal fold visualisation (94.3% vs 96.1%, p = 0.29), sensitivity (58.3% vs 65.7%, p = 0.5) and specificity (97.8% vs 98.4%) did not differ significantly, and sensitivity improved with operator experience. Point-of-care devices may broaden implementation of TLUSG in perioperative and outpatient settings.

Does Transcutaneous Laryngeal Ultrasonography Improve Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs) Compared to Flexible Laryngoscopy? - A Cross-Sectional Survey Study.

Patel A, Spychalski P, Albrahim M, Markiet K, Kuryłowicz J, Mikaszewski B, et al. Journal of Voice 2026. Original study · DOI 10.1016/j.jvoice.2026.03.031 · PubMed 41997784

Prospective cross-sectional survey nested in a learning-curve study, in which patients undergoing elective thyroidectomy (n = 96; 116 responses) rated transcutaneous laryngeal ultrasonography (TLUSG) and laryngoscopy using a purpose-designed 12-item questionnaire. TLUSG had lower composite burden scores (median 7 vs 20.5, p < 0.001), was preferred by 84.4% of patients, and all patients were willing to repeat it compared with 82.3% for laryngoscopy (p < 0.001); comfort was lower in men and in patients with vocal fold palsy. The findings support considering TLUSG in patient-centred perioperative vocal fold screening.

Learning Curve Analysis of Conventional and Point-of-Care Transcutaneous Laryngeal Ultrasonography.

Patel A, Spychalski P, Albrahim M, Markiet K, Kuryłowicz J, Mikaszewski B, et al. Journal of Voice 2026. Original study · DOI 10.1016/j.jvoice.2026.03.004 · PubMed 41936512

Prospective cohort (Gdańsk, January to August 2022) in which six ultrasound-naive medical students performed transcutaneous laryngeal ultrasonography with point-of-care and conventional devices in thyroidectomy patients, with laryngoscopy as reference; learning curves were analysed with CUSUM, segmented regression and LC-CUSUM. Across 120 examinations the visualisation rate was 94.9%; the learning phase for time to visualisation and to complete examination ended at the 7th and 5th examination for point-of-care and the 24th and 16th for conventional ultrasound, and diagnostic proficiency was reached between the 7th and 15th examination. Proficiency was acquired around the 25th examination, supporting feasibility of bedside implementation.

Single Nucleotide Polymorphisms as Biomarkers of Response to Neoadjuvant Chemoradiotherapy in Rectal Cancer: A Systematic Review.

Połomska K, Rybicka M, Jażdżewska A, Prud M, Jackowska S, Kobiela J, Spychalski P. Cancers 2025. Systematic review · senior (last) author · DOI 10.3390/cancers17243995 · PubMed 41463244

Systematic review (PRISMA; PubMed and Web of Science) of 32 studies including 4116 patients, evaluating 304 single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) across 126 genes as predictors of response to neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy in rectal cancer. DNA repair (XRCC1, XRCC3, ERCC1, ERCC2) and folate metabolism genes (MTHFR, TYMS) were most studied; only rs25487 (XRCC1) and rs1801133 (MTHFR) showed predictive value in more than one study, with inconsistent associations, and no SNP was validated across independent cohorts. Current evidence does not support clinical use of individual SNPs; large prospective studies and polygenic or multi-omic models are proposed.

Bioimpedance Measurement for Monitoring Chronic Wounds: A Systematic Review.

Antoszewska M, Połomska K, Spychalski P, Kekonen A, Viik J, Barańska-Rybak W. International Wound Journal 2025. Systematic review · DOI 10.1111/iwj.70707 · PubMed 40505690

Systematic review (PRISMA; PubMed, Scopus and Web of Science, searched August 2024) of 16 studies on bioimpedance measurement for monitoring chronic lower leg wounds. Included studies consistently showed significant bioimpedance differences between wound and healthy skin and changes over the healing process, with wide heterogeneity in electrode types and configurations. Bioimpedance appears potentially useful for quantitative wound monitoring, but methodological challenges remain before clinical implementation.

Effectiveness of Open Incision Negative Pressure Wound Therapy for Surgical Site Infection Prevention for Abdominal Surgery-A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis.

Połomska K, Kowalik J, Kobiela J, Spychalski P. International Wound Journal 2025. Meta-analysis · senior (last) author · DOI 10.1111/iwj.70199 · PubMed 40234096

Systematic review and random-effects meta-analysis (PROSPERO CRD42024401669) of 8 studies (3 randomised trials, 5 cohort studies; 1655 patients) on open incision negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT) in abdominal surgery, 5 of which were pooled. NPWT was associated with lower surgical site infection risk than primary closure (OR 0.15, 95% CI 0.02 to 0.87), with no significant difference compared with delayed primary closure (OR 0.28, 95% CI 0.06 to 1.27). Standardised treatment and reporting protocols are needed to evaluate NPWT effectiveness.

Comparison of Staging and Treatment of Rectal Cancer between Patients Younger and Older than 70 years - An International Multicenter Study.

Krzeszowiak J, Duta C, Pach RA, Richter P, Lorenc Z, Rutkowski A, et al. (Spychalski P 22/25). Chirurgia (Bucharest) 2025. Original study · DOI 10.21614/chirurgia.3108 · PubMed 40367306

Retrospective analysis of prospectively collected data from nine centres in Poland, Romania and Germany including 2443 patients operated on for rectal cancer between 2013 and 2019, compared by age (under vs at least 70 years). Staging, sex and tumour location did not differ, but older patients more often had stoma formation, had lower lymph node yield and less frequent preoperative radiotherapy, with no difference in R0 resection or complete response rates. Overall survival was significantly lower in older patients in stages I to IV, indicating age-related differences in treatment intensity.

Technical Aspects of Transcutaneous Laryngeal Ultrasonography: A Review.

Patel A, Spychalski P, Połomska K, Albrahim M, Markiet K, Kuryłowicz J, et al. Journal of Voice 2025. Review · DOI 10.1016/j.jvoice.2025.01.040 · PubMed 40102161

Technical review of transcutaneous laryngeal ultrasonography (TLUSG) for peri-thyroidectomy vocal fold assessment. It describes identification of the false vocal folds, true vocal folds and arytenoid cartilages via the anterior (transverse) or lateral (sagittal) approach, with passive breathing, phonation and the Valsalva manoeuvre to elicit movement. The paper provides a step-by-step algorithm, common pitfalls and modifications to support adoption by surgeons, anaesthesiologists and endocrinologists.

Adenoma Detection Rates by Physicians and Subsequent Colorectal Cancer Risk.

Pilonis ND, Spychalski P, Kalager M, Løberg M, Wieszczy P, Didkowska J, et al. JAMA 2025. Original study · DOI 10.1001/jama.2024.22975 · PubMed 39680377

Observational study of 789 physicians and 485,615 patients in the Polish Colonoscopy Screening Program (2000 to 2017, median follow-up 10.2 years; 1873 colorectal cancers), using joinpoint regression to relate change in adenoma detection rate (ADR) to postcolonoscopy colorectal cancer incidence. A change in trend occurred at an ADR of 26%; among physicians with baseline ADR below 26%, improvement was associated with lower incidence (31.8 vs 40.7 per 100,000 person-years; difference 8.9, 95% CI 5.06 to 12.74), whereas above 26% no difference was observed (23.4 vs 22.5; difference 0.9, 95% CI -6.46 to 8.26). ADR improvement appears relevant mainly for endoscopists below this threshold.

Preoperative stoma site marking: nurses' perspective

Leyk-Kolańczak M, Spychalski P, Kruk A, Terech-Skóra S, Książek J, Dobosz M, et al. Pielęgniarstwo Chirurgiczne i Angiologiczne / Surgical and Vascular Nursing 2024. Original study · DOI 10.5114/.2024.141477

Survey of 164 Polish nurses recruited at three nursing conferences on perceived practice of preoperative stoma site marking. Forty percent reported that marking is always performed before elective surgery on their ward and 30% that it is performed only sometimes; marking before emergency surgery was confirmed by 14.6%, and the main barrier was absence of a ward protocol (82.3%), followed by lack of trained staff, surgeon restrictions and time. Implementation in Poland remains unsatisfactory, supporting training of ostomy nurses and a uniform national procedure.

Mortality and rate of hospitalization stratified by patients' BMI in a colonoscopy screening - a cross sectional analysis of data from Polish Colonoscopy Screening Platform.

Spychalski P, Wieszczy P, Połomska K, Kobiela J, Regula J, Kaminski MF, et al. Scandinavian Journal of Gastroenterology 2024. Original study · first author · DOI 10.1080/00365521.2024.2410795 · PubMed 39363857

Retrospective cohort of 55,390 individuals screened in the Polish Colonoscopy Screening Program (2012 to 2015), comparing mortality and unplanned hospitalisation from 42 days before to 30 days after colonoscopy across BMI categories, with adjustment and subanalyses by sex and procedure type. Obese individuals had more hospitalisations than non-obese individuals (1.94% vs 0%, p = 0.038), particularly obese men, while unadjusted and adjusted mortality did not differ by BMI. Overweight and obesity were not clinically relevant risk factors for mortality or hospitalisation around screening colonoscopy.

Bioimpedance sensor array for monitoring chronic wounds: Validation of method feasibility.

Antoszewska M, Spychalski P, Kekonen A, Viik J, Barańska-Rybak W. International Wound Journal 2024. Original study · DOI 10.1111/iwj.14899 · PubMed 39099180

Single-centre observational feasibility study of a multielectrode sensor array for bioimpedance measurement in chronic lower leg wounds of venous, mixed venous-arterial and diabetic aetiology. In 104 measurements of 18 ulcers (7 patients analysed), bioimpedance increased as wound area decreased, with a strong negative correlation (r = -0.86, p < 0.001). The method is feasible for quantitative wound monitoring; long-term studies are needed to establish usability of the electrode dressing.

The impact of oncological package implementation on the treatment of rectal cancer in years 2013-2019 in Poland - multicenter study.

Krzeszowiak J, Pach R, Richter P, Lorenc Z, Rutkowski A, Ochwat K, et al. (Spychalski P 24/25). Polish Journal of Surgery (Polski Przegląd Chirurgiczny) 2024. Original study · DOI 10.5604/01.3001.0054.2680 · PubMed 38940243

Multicentre retrospective analysis of 1418 patients operated on for rectal cancer in five Polish centres (2013 to 2019), comparing periods before, early after and later after introduction of the 2015 oncological package (fast-track pathway with mandatory multidisciplinary team meetings). Across periods, local T stage decreased, median lymph node yield increased in the latest period, and use of preoperative (especially short-course) radiotherapy rose, including among patients with T3/4 or N+ M0 disease. The association with the oncological package is indirect and requires further data.

Recommendations on the use of prehabilitation, i.e. comprehensive preparation of the patient for surgery.

Banasiewicz T, Kobiela J, Cwaliński J, Spychalski P, Przybylska P, Kornacka K, et al. Polish Journal of Surgery (Polski Przegląd Chirurgiczny) 2023. Guideline · DOI 10.5604/01.3001.0053.8854 · PubMed 38348849

Multidisciplinary Polish expert recommendations on prehabilitation before surgery, covering indications, scope, duration and methods of delivery. Core components include nutritional optimisation, exercise, psychological support and addiction cessation, together with correction of anaemia, glycaemic control, stabilisation of comorbidities and procedure-specific preparation (for example, ostomy). Assessment of Calcium Score Cutoff Point for Clinically Significant Aortic Stenosis on Lung Cancer Screening Program Low-Dose Computed Tomography: A Cross-Sectional Analysis

Klein-Awerjanow K, Rzyman W, Dziedzic R, Fijałkowska J, Spychalski P, Szurowska E, et al. Diagnostics 2023. Original study · DOI 10.3390/diagnostics13020246 · PubMed 36673055

Cross-sectional analysis of 6631 heavy smokers in the MOLTEST BIS lung cancer screening programme, with Agatston aortic valve calcium scoring on LDCT; participants with a calcium score of 900 or more (n = 54) underwent echocardiography. ROC analysis identified a cutoff of 1758 for at least moderate aortic stenosis (sensitivity 85.71%, specificity 75.76%) and 2665 for severe stenosis (sensitivity 87.5%, specificity 76.92%). Calcium scoring on screening LDCT may allow opportunistic detection of clinically significant aortic stenosis.

Triiodothyronine lowers the potential of colorectal cancer stem cells in vitro

Rostkowska O, Olejniczak-Kęder A, Spychalski P, Szaryńska M, Kobiela J. Oncology Reports 2023. Original study · DOI 10.3892/or.2022.8458 · PubMed 36484405

In vitro study of colonospheres derived from two colorectal cancer cell lines incubated with triiodothyronine (T3). T3 reduced viability, proliferation and spherogenic potential (P<0.05), increased apoptosis, shifted cells towards G0/G1 arrest and reduced sphere size, with effects on THR alpha 1, THR beta 1, DIO2 and DIO3 expression depending on cell line and clinical stage of origin. The findings suggest a role for thyroid hormone signalling in regulating colorectal cancer stem cells and a possible therapeutic target.

Comparison of the Long-term Outcomes of RYGB and OAGB as Conversion Procedures After Failed LSG: a Case-Control Study

Wilczyński M, Spychalski P, Proczko-Stepaniak M, Bigda J, Szymański M, Dobrzycka M, et al. Journal of Gastrointestinal Surgery 2022. Original study · DOI 10.1007/s11605-022-05395-w · PubMed 35790676

Case-control study from a prospectively maintained database of 80 patients converted after failed laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (33 RYGB, 47 OAGB). At 5 years mean %EBWL was 84.04% after RYGB vs 72.95% after OAGB (p = 0.2176); complete type 2 diabetes remission was more frequent after OAGB (97.3% vs 33%; p = 0.035), with no significant differences in other comorbidity remission or Comprehensive Complication Index; 7 patients developed de novo GERD after OAGB and none after RYGB. Both procedures appear acceptable as conversion options, with OAGB favoured for diabetes remission.

Adenoma to Colorectal Cancer Estimated Transition Rates Stratified by BMI Categories: A Cross-Sectional Analysis of Asymptomatic Individuals from Screening Colonoscopy Program

Spychalski P, Kobiela J, Wieszczy P, Bugajski M, Reguła J, Kaminski MF. Cancers 2022. Original study · first author, corresponding author · DOI 10.3390/cancers14010062 · PubMed 35008224

Cross-sectional analysis of 147,385 participants of the Polish Colonoscopy Screening Program, with prevalence adjusted for sex, age, family history, smoking, diabetes and medication use, and estimated transition rates (eTR) calculated as ratios of adjusted prevalences across BMI strata (normal, overweight, obese). Prevalence of non-advanced adenomas (12.19%, 13.81%, 14.70%) and advanced adenomas (5.20%, 5.77%, 6.61%) rose with BMI (p < 0.001), while eTR from early to advanced cancer was lowest in obese individuals (73.73%, 69.90%, 50.54%). The data suggest an obesity paradox in the adenoma to carcinoma sequence.

Proton pump inhibitors and colorectal cancer: A systematic review

Patel A, Spychalski P, Antoszewska M, Regula J, Kobiela J. World Journal of Gastroenterology 2021. Systematic review · DOI 10.3748/wjg.v27.i44.7716 · PubMed 34908809

PRISMA-based systematic review of MEDLINE, EMBASE, Scopus and Web of Science to May 2021, including 28 studies (12 basic research, 11 epidemiological, 5 treatment studies). Basic research suggests PPIs do not promote and may inhibit colorectal carcinogenesis, epidemiological data do not support a causal association with increased cancer risk, and treatment studies suggest concomitant PPI use may reduce capecitabine efficacy, while pantoprazole may act as a chemosensitiser with FOLFOX. Agent-specific effects warrant prospective evaluation.

Transcutaneous Laryngeal Ultrasound for Vocal Cord Paralysis Assessment in Patients Undergoing Thyroid and Parathyroid Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Patel A, Spychalski P, Aszkiełowicz A, Mikaszewski B, Kobiela J. Journal of Clinical Medicine 2021. Meta-analysis · DOI 10.3390/jcm10225393 · PubMed 34830675

Systematic review and meta-analysis (PRISMA) of 16 studies assessing transcutaneous laryngeal ultrasound against laryngoscopy for vocal cord assessment around thyroid and parathyroid surgery. Pooled visualisation rates were 86.28% preoperatively and 94.13% postoperatively; postoperative pooled sensitivity was 83.96% (95% CI 77.24 to 88.50%) and specificity 96.15% (95% CI 95.24 to 96.88%), with male sex and older age as main risk factors for non-visualisation. The method may serve as a screening tool, potentially avoiding laryngoscopy in about 80% of patients.

Neoadjuvant short-course radiotherapy with consolidation chemotherapy for locally advanced rectal cancer: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Spychalski P, Patel A, Corrao G, Jereczek-Fossa BA, Glynne-Jones R, Garcia-Aguilar J, et al. Acta Oncologica 2021. Meta-analysis · co-first author · DOI 10.1080/0284186X.2021.1953137 · PubMed 34308767

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 11 studies (6 randomised, 5 non-randomised; seven cohorts) comparing short-course radiotherapy with consolidation chemotherapy (SCRT-CCT) and long-course chemoradiotherapy (LCRT). SCRT-CCT increased pathological complete response (RR 1.75, 95% CI 1.41 to 2.19) without a significant difference in 3-year overall survival (RR 1.06, 95% CI 0.98 to 1.14), with comparable R0 resection, downstaging, compliance and late toxicity but higher acute toxicity. Protocol heterogeneity limits firm conclusions pending ongoing trials.

Obesity in work-up of kidney transplant candidates: review of clinical practice guidelines

Dobrzycka M, Spychalski P, Łącka M, Proczko-Stepaniak M, Dębska-Ślizień A, Kaska Ł, et al. European Journal of Translational and Clinical Medicine 2020. Review · DOI 10.31373/ejtcm/128771

Costs of elective vs emergency cholecystectomy in diabetic patients

Łącka M, Spychalski P, Obłój P, Łaski D, Rostkowska O, Wieszczy P, et al. European Journal of Translational and Clinical Medicine 2020. Original study · DOI 10.31373/ejtcm/128963

Single-centre retrospective analysis of age and sex-matched diabetic and non-diabetic patients undergoing elective or emergency cholecystectomy in Poland (2016 to 2019). Elective cholecystectomy costs were similar (739.31 vs 797.14 USD, p = 0.51), whereas emergency cholecystectomy cost more in diabetic patients (3950.72 vs 2464.31 USD, p = 0.04); emergency surgery was significantly costlier than elective in both groups. Early elective referral of diabetic patients with gallstone disease may reduce costs.

Assessment of nutritional status of patients with cancer who are qualified for home enteral nutrition: a retrospective analysis

Kaźmierczak-Siedlecka K, Folwarski M, Jankowska B, Spychalski P, et al. European Journal of Translational and Clinical Medicine 2020. Original study · DOI 10.31373/ejtcm/120583

Retrospective analysis of 84 patients with cancer qualified for home enteral nutrition at a nutritional counselling centre in Gdańsk (2009 to 2015), assessed by body mass index, serum total protein and albumin, total lymphocyte count and Nutritional Risk Score 2002. Hypoalbuminaemia was more frequent in gastric than oesophageal cancer (p = 0.02), low lymphocyte count was present in 47.6%, and all patients scored at least 3 points on NRS 2002 (most often 5 points, 40.4%). All patients required nutritional treatment, although nutritional status varied.

Circulating Tumor DNA in KRAS positive colorectal cancer patients as a prognostic factor - a systematic review and meta-analysis

Spychalski P, Perdyan A, Kacperczyk J, Rostkowska O, Kobiela J. Critical Reviews in Oncology/Hematology 2020. Meta-analysis · co-first author · DOI 10.1016/j.critrevonc.2020.103065 · PubMed 32763752

PRISMA systematic review of PubMed, Web of Science and Scopus including 18 studies (1779 patients) on KRAS-mutant circulating tumour DNA in serum or plasma of colorectal cancer patients. Six of 8 studies linked ctDNA presence, and all 6 studies linked high ctDNA concentration, with inferior overall survival. KRAS ctDNA appears to be a negative prognostic factor; concentration cut-offs are needed before clinical implementation.

Clinical presentation and outcomes of cholecystectomy for acute cholecystitis in patients with diabetes - A matched pair analysis. A pilot study

Łącka M, Obłój P, Spychalski P, Łaski D, Rostkowska O, Wieszczy P, et al. Advances in Medical Sciences 2020. Original study · DOI 10.1016/j.advms.2020.08.001 · PubMed 32823170

Retrospective pilot matched-pair analysis of 267 patients undergoing emergency cholecystectomy for acute cholecystitis (2007 to 2017), with 197 age- and sex-matched controls without diabetes (3:1). Symptoms at admission did not differ, but patients with diabetes had significantly higher operative and postoperative complication rates, longer operative time and hospital stay, and more frequent open surgery, without a higher conversion rate.
